ApDepth-G: Multi-Step Geometric Diffusion Refinement for Robust Monocular Depth Estimation

Project · Paper · Code

Abstract

Diffusion-based depth estimators can assign inconsistent, finite depth to sky regions and introduce false structures from cloud textures. ApDepth-G addresses this failure through progressive geometric refinement guided by an RGB image and a pretrained depth prior. Its multi-step denoising process repeatedly corrects the latent depth representation. Training combines offset-noise regularization, signal-to-noise-ratio-based loss weighting, and latent gradient consistency to stabilize optimization and preserve structural boundaries. Sky-aware refinement further suppresses unreliable responses in ambiguous regions. The abstract reports reduced sky-depth collapse in challenging outdoor scenes. The introduction positions this approach as a complement to ApDepth: single-step prediction emphasizes efficiency, while iterative refinement trades some speed for more opportunities to correct uncertain geometry.

ApDepth: Aiming for Precise Monocular Depth Estimation Based on Diffusion Models

Article info

Authors
  • Jiawei Wang
  • Mingbo Lei
  • Haoze Shou
  • Yusu Liang
  • Yibo Chen
Keywords
  • Diffusion Models
  • Monocular Depth Estimation
  • Two-Stage Training
  • Frequency-Domain Learning

Project · Paper · Code

Abstract

ApDepth is a single-step diffusion framework for monocular depth estimation that, in the paper's words, enables fast inference while preserving fine-grained edge details. It addresses the trade-off between the latency of iterative denoising and the loss of local boundaries in single-step prediction. A pretrained depth estimator supplies semantic priors and global structure. A coarse-to-fine, two-stage training process then restores detail: cosine-similarity feature alignment connects intermediate U-Net features with an auxiliary encoder, while an iteration-based loss schedule moves from latent MSE and pixel L1 constraints to frequency-domain FFT supervision. The spatial losses establish global depth structure; the final frequency-based phase targets sharp edges. The paper reports competitive or improved benchmark performance while substantially accelerating inference.
